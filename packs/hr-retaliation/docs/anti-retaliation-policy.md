# POL-HR-07: Anti-Retaliation Policy

Cedarline Outfitters (fictional). Applies to every employee, with added duties for people managers.

## 1 Why this policy exists

We can only fix problems we hear about. Employees will only speak up if they trust nothing bad will happen to them for doing so. Retaliation breaks that trust, even when the original concern turns out to be unfounded.

## 2 What is protected

### 2.1 Protected activity

Protected activity includes raising a good-faith concern about conduct, safety, pay, or treatment; taking part in an investigation as a witness; and supporting a coworker who raised a concern.

### 2.2 Good faith

A concern is in good faith if the employee honestly believes it is true. A concern that turns out to be wrong is still protected. Knowingly false reports are handled separately by the People Team.

## 3 What retaliation looks like

### 3.1 Obvious actions

Firing, demotion, cutting hours or pay, and a sudden poor performance review are retaliation when they happen because of protected activity.

### 3.2 Subtle actions

Retaliation can also be quiet: moving someone to worse shifts, leaving them out of meetings or team events, giving them the silent treatment, or telling others they are a problem. If it could discourage a reasonable employee from speaking up, it counts.

### 3.3 Timing matters

An adverse action soon after a concern is raised will be looked at closely. Before any discipline, schedule change, or review rating for an employee who raised a concern in the last 12 months, a manager must check with the People Team first.

## 4 Manager duties

### 4.1 Do not investigate yourself

Managers do not investigate concerns. They listen, document, and hand off to the People Team.

### 4.2 Report within one business day

A manager who receives a concern, or sees possible retaliation, must report it to the People Team within 1 business day.

### 4.3 Confidentiality

Concerns are kept as private as possible and shared only with people who need to know. Managers must not promise complete confidentiality, because the People Team may need to speak with others to resolve the concern.

## 5 Reporting channels

Employees can raise a concern with their manager, any other manager, the People Team, or the 24-hour Speak Up line. They do not have to go to their own manager first.

## 6 Consequences

Anyone who retaliates faces discipline up to and including termination, regardless of their role.
