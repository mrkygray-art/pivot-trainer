# GUIDE-HR-12: Responding When an Employee Raises a Concern

Cedarline Outfitters (fictional). A step-by-step guide for people managers. Read with POL-HR-07.

## 1 In the moment

### 1.1 Make time and space

If an employee says they want to raise a concern, move to a private place as soon as you reasonably can. If you cannot talk right then, set a time within the same day.

### 1.2 Listen first

Let the employee finish. Do not interrupt, argue, or defend the person they are describing. Ask open questions such as "What happened next?" and "Who else was there?"

### 1.3 Thank them

Thank the employee for raising it. Say clearly that raising a concern will not be held against them.

### 1.4 What not to say

Do not promise a specific outcome, do not promise complete confidentiality, and do not say whether you believe the concern.

## 2 Right after the conversation

### 2.1 Write it down

Within the same day, write down what the employee told you, in their words where possible, with the date, time, and anyone they named. Stick to facts and leave out your opinions.

### 2.2 Hand off to the People Team

Send your notes to the People Team within 1 business day using the Concern Intake form. Do not contact the people named in the concern.

### 2.3 Tell the employee what happens next

Let the employee know you have passed the concern to the People Team and that someone from the People Team will follow up with them.

## 3 In the weeks after

### 3.1 Treat them the same

Keep the employee's schedule, assignments, and inclusion the same as before. Watch for coworkers excluding them.

### 3.2 Check before any change

Before any discipline, schedule change, or review rating for this employee, check with the People Team first (see POL-HR-07, section 3.3).

### 3.3 Check in

Two weeks after the concern, check in with the employee privately: "How are things going for you on the team?" Report any signs of retaliation to the People Team within 1 business day.
